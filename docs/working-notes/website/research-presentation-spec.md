## 1. The job to recover

Martin is a son helping his mother and a professional building her website. He went
beyond implementation to investigate how the website could help her business: attract
new leads beyond recommendations, demonstrate the capacity for more substantial and
higher-budget suitable commissions, develop professional work, and address the questions
that keep people from understanding or commissioning her services. Smaller missions
remain welcome. The research also explored discovery, visits and buying for the Galerie.

He wants Crystelle to understand what he investigated, what he found, why it matters,
and the possible responses for Jukkai. Only then should the presentation explain how
they can work together to turn that knowledge and her professional experience into
useful website content. This is preparation for the fuller website beyond the magazine
edition, not a retrospective limited to the landing and contact pages.

The first package had usable sources and some accurate recommendations, but often
replaced findings with general advice about content, scope and proof. Martin could not
recover the business argument from the slides. Reconstruct that argument; changing
colours or simplifying sentences will not repair it.

The organising relationship is:

**Business question → the searches and investigations used → actual findings and
examples → what we learn → possible responses for Jukkai.**

After the complete research picture:

**What content could help → what we already know → what to explore with Crystelle →
usable material → possible places on the website.**

Do not turn search estimates into promised leads, higher budgets or sector profitability.
The research informs choices; it does not settle new commercial priorities. Explain
what is known, what Martin recommends and what still needs Crystelle's experience in
ordinary language, without repeatedly announcing that nothing is approved.

## 2. Deliverables and voice

Produce three connected outputs from the same substantive argument:

- **French Google Slides with French speaker notes:** native and editable, with a
  clear spoken path of roughly 40 minutes, flexible toward an hour when useful.
  No fixed slide count. Martin should understand and be able to present the material
  without rereading the preparation chats or becoming an SEO lecturer.
- **French Google Doc and PDF:** the same research argument with enough explanation,
  figures, examples and references to stand alone. No page target or padding. It is
  a research explanation, with a short closing bridge explaining what Martin and
  Crystelle will work on together and why. Detailed interview instructions stay in
  the separate kit.
- **Portable interview kit:** one reusable master interviewer prompt and prefilled
  topic context packs for focused conversations with Crystelle. Private local Markdown
  is suitable; make the contents easy to copy into a fresh AI conversation. Include
  brief usage instructions. It need not become another Google document.

Write as Martin speaking to his mother: relaxed, professional, direct and specific.
Use natural French, including « je » and « tu » where appropriate. Explain unfamiliar
terms when they help; prefer “les personnes qui cherchent…” to abstract audience jargon.
Use real questions, choices and examples. A paragraph claiming that a reference
“connects a constraint to a decision” must actually identify the constraint and decision.

Avoid corporate executive summaries, management language, formal annex stacks and
repeated caveats. An opening explanation can be short and human: why Martin looked
into this, what business questions he followed and what they will learn today.
Do not manufacture Martin's memories, personal anecdotes or Crystelle's answers.

The report may use a useful linked contents list and a compact source section. Run
labels such as “R35” are internal traceability aids, not reader-facing explanations.
References must name the site/page, show what was observed and provide a usable link.
Do not make a reader travel through a source appendix to understand a claim.
