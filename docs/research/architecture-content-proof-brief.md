# Architecture content and proof — working brief

**Status: agent proposal for discussion, 2026-09-10.** This translates reviewed
research into possible content. It is not approved copy, a sitemap, a new offer,
or a release requirement. Martin owns page and creative choices; Crystelle's actual
work and mission terms supply the claims. [Current delivery](../operations/current-delivery.md)
owns timing and [the foundation](../strategy/foundation.md) owns business truth.

## What the research adds

**Direct business context from Martin, September 10:** price and budget are the
main factors in recent enquiries. On larger projects, a much less frequent concern
is whether to work directly with an architect, including around permits. Martin
confirms that Crystelle is used to collaborating with architects when needed.
This is his account of actual enquiries, not a measured conversion study or a
statement of permitting law.

Martin clarified that the price concern covers **both works and Crystelle's fees**.
Many people imagine their budget funding a finished, designed interior, then learn
that necessary electricity, plumbing, insulation or other underlying work can
leave much less for visible changes. This is the main expectation gap to explain;
the required work and allocation depend on the actual property and mission.

[Runs 37–40](seo-research-log.md#37-residential-renovation-vocabulary--which-larger-project-needs-deserve-closer-research)
connect broader renovation needs to local help, project evidence and professional
selection. The two renovation SERPs differ on page one; the selection SERP includes
practice-authored guidance. Neither pattern measures Jukkai enquiries or establishes
an easy ranking opportunity. Other selection questions are hypotheses about visitor
needs, not validated personas or equal priorities. Martin's account gives budget
greater practical weight than the breadth of topics in competitor selection guides.

The website should help a visitor recognise a relevant project, understand what
Crystelle contributes and decide whether to start a conversation. This applies
both to a referred visitor checking the practice and to someone discovering a
project or service through search.

## A possible reading sequence

1. Introduce the practice and relevant transformations, supported by selected work.
2. Explain scope and budget, separating works from professional remuneration and
   showing the design contribution through concrete decisions and outputs.
3. Clarify collaboration, including how an architect may join where needed.
4. Explain the actual first conversation and how timing and feasibility are assessed.

This is a sequence to test, not four mandatory sections or separate URLs. Proof
can appear beside the relevant explanation. The homepage's relationship to a
dedicated architecture page, residential/B2B treatment and Galerie balance remain
open; this brief does not settle those wider choices.

## Budget explanation — draft direction

Help visitors understand how the same envelope covers required underlying work,
visible transformation and the professional mission. Avoid implying a fixed split
or that every project needs the same technical work. Explain the contribution of
design and support through actual choices, without promising that fees pay for
themselves or that every desired finish can fit the initial budget.

**Illustrative French copy for discussion, not approved publication text:**

> Rénover un intérieur, c'est aussi intervenir sur ce qui ne se voit pas. Selon
> l'état du logement, l'électricité, la plomberie ou l'isolation peuvent mobiliser
> une part importante du budget. Les travaux, les aménagements et les honoraires de
> la mission doivent être envisagés ensemble, pour choisir les transformations qui
> comptent le plus pour vous.

Support this with one real example: the starting condition, necessary work, the
client's priorities and the resulting choices. If figures are available and cleared,
show dated, scoped amounts with fees distinguished; otherwise explain the decisions
without invented numbers. A simple case can demonstrate why budget and design
must be discussed together. It does not require a calculator or a public tariff grid.

## Next evidence to make the draft concrete

Use one substantial residential project, one professional-space project and, where
ready, one smaller mission to test whether the explanations work across the offer.
For each, capture the client's starting problem, what Crystelle actually did, one
important choice and the material available to show it. These are proposed samples,
not required new case-study pages or prerequisites to the first release.

Martin has identified the budget expectation gap. A useful next example would show
what the client expected to achieve, which underlying work was necessary, how fees
were explained and what choices were made. An anonymised account is enough for
internal learning; no new pricing offer follows from it.

For the less frequent architect question, select an actual collaboration if a
publishable example exists: explain who designed, who handled the relevant formal
work and how responsibilities were divided. General legal-role comparisons cannot
substitute for Crystelle's real scope and the other professional's involvement.

Then draft the selected content using confirmed facts, with unresolved fee, scope,
timing and proof details visibly left open. Volume, difficulty and backlinks would
help assess a proposed standalone acquisition article; they do not answer these
business-content questions. Broader B2B, Galerie and regional research can continue.
